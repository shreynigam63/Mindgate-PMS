# Agentic PMS — Manager Manual

**Mindgate Performance Management System**
For anyone with people reporting to them. Version of 30 September 2026.

---

## 1. Signing in

Same as everyone else: your work email, and the password HR gave you. **The
first time you sign in the system stops and makes you choose your own
password** — there is no way past that screen, and the new one must be at
least 8 characters. Change it later with **Password**, top right. There is no
self-service reset; HR does resets.

**You will see at least two tabs across the top: `Self` and `Manager`.** Your
own KRAs, your own appraisal and your own timesheet live under **Self** — you
are an employee too, and the Employee Manual covers that half. This manual
covers the **Manager** tab.

If you also head a department you will see a third tab, **HOD**,
which is a different job: acting on a whole department rather than on your own
reports. It sits behind its own permission.

## 2. The Manager menu

| Menu item | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Manager Dashboard** | Everything your reports owe, and everything they are waiting on **you** for. |
| **Team Overview** | One grid: where every report stands on every stage. |
| **Team KRA Sheets** | Approve, return or edit your reports' KRA sheets. |
| **Team Target Achievements** | Your reports' target achievements for the year. |
| **Team Mid-Year** | Their mid-year checkpoints. |
| **Team Evaluation** | Where you write the annual evaluation. |
| **Team Competencies** | Assess each report against what their role needs. |
| **Timesheet** | Their compliance, and the KRA mapping session. |

**Connects** is not in this list — it sits under **Self**, because
the page is two-sided: the same screen shows the connects where you are the
employee and the ones where you are the manager.

**Start at Manager Dashboard.** It is the only screen that tells you what is
blocked *on you*. Everything else is a place you go once it has sent you
there.

## 3. Team KRA Sheets — approving the year's goals

This is the first thing you owe your team in a new cycle. Nothing else in the
year works until their KRAs exist.

Open **Team KRA Sheets**, pick a report, and read what they have written. You
have three choices:

- **Approve** — the sheet is agreed and the cycle can proceed on it.
- **Return for edits** — sends it back with **your comment**. Write a real instruction, not "please revise": the comment is the only thing they see telling them what to fix, and it shows on their screen as *"Your last comment"*.
- **Save changes** — you can **edit a submitted sheet directly** rather than bouncing it back. Use this for small corrections you would only have to explain twice. Use *Return for edits* when the thinking needs to change.

**Weights must total 100.** If a report has pulled KRAs from their role's
published shelf, remember the shelf is a menu and does not itself total 100 —
they pick a hundred points' worth from it. Check the total before approving.

**Look at what is measurable.** A KRA with no metric and no data source is one
you will both argue about in March. The KPI column is where that gets settled
now.

## 4. Team Overview, Target Achievements, Mid-Year

**Team Overview** is a single grid of your reports against the stages of the
cycle — KRAs, Connects, Dev Plan, Aspiring Career, Annual Review, Manager Eval
— with *Not set* where nothing exists yet. Use it to find the gaps; use the
Dashboard to find what is yours to clear.

**Team Target Achievements** shows what each report is aiming at for the year
and the role they want next. Worth reading before a Connect.

**Team Mid-Year** is the halfway checkpoint against each KRA. Your reports
rate and comment on themselves; you do the same. **The overall is computed
from the per-KRA ratings — you do not type it.** That is deliberate: the
overall should follow from the parts, and it stays traceable to them.

**Connects** (under Self) records the conversation: achievements,
blockers, feedback, and which KRAs it touched. Both of you can see and add to
it. Keeping these current is what makes the annual evaluation a summary rather
than an invention.

## 5. Team Evaluation — the annual assessment

Open a report and you get their annual review beside your evaluation form.

**What is on the screen:**

- **Their write-up**, and their **self-rating**, per KRA — so you are rating the same thing they wrote about, not your memory of the year.
- **Rate each KRA.** The **overall rating is computed from these**, not typed.
- **Strengths** and **Improvement areas**.
- **Potential** — a separate judgement from performance. It feeds the 9-Box grid HR uses, so it is not a second performance score.
- **Submit evaluation** when it is finished.

**The AI drafting button — "Copy into fields (then edit)".** The system can
draft strengths and improvement areas from what it already holds. Two things
about it, and please take them seriously:

- **It is a draft, and it is labelled as one.** It copies into the fields precisely so you edit it. Anything left unedited goes out under your name, about a real person, into a record that affects their increment.
- **It never sets a number.** Every rating, score and distribution in this system is computed or typed by a human. The AI writes sentences and nothing else.

Your work saves as you go (**Saving… / Saved ✓**), so a lost connection does
not lose the paragraph you just wrote.

## 6. Team Competencies

Assess each report against what their role requires, with an example of what
"good" looks like at each level. They rate themselves separately, and **the
screen shows you where your view differs from theirs** — which is the useful
part, and the thing worth talking about in the next Connect rather than
discovering at year end.

## 7. Timesheet — compliance, and the mapping session

Two tabs: **Compliance** and **KRA coverage**. Click any report to open their
full record.

### Compliance

Whether your reports filled their timesheets, with a Red / Amber / Green
marker. Chasing this is ordinary management work and needs no explanation.

### KRA coverage — the mapping session

**This is the screen that is yours and nobody else's.** Your reports can see
every number on it but cannot change any of it; HR can see the backlog but
does not do the mapping. **The manager decides which KRA each logged item
serves**, because you are the only person who knows.

Open a report, choose **KRA coverage**, and work down **"What was logged — and
which KRA it serves"**. Each item has a dropdown:

- **Pick the KRA this serves** — the hours go to that KRA.
- **Not KRA work** — the hours are deliberately left out of the coverage figure. **This asks you for a reason on the screen** (*"Why is this not KRA work?"*) and will not save without one. That is on purpose: excluded hours disappear from someone's coverage, and a year later the reason is the only thing that explains it.
- **undo** clears a pending choice.

Then **Save N mappings**. Two details:

- **The chip on an item shows the *saved* state, not your draft.** A pending choice is labelled unsaved until you press save.
- **"Saved together — if one is refused, none of them is written."** The batch is all-or-nothing, so you never end up with half a session applied.

### What the numbers mean

| Number | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Hours placed** | How much logged time is attached to a KRA. |
| **KRA coverage** | How much of the sheet, **by weight, not by count**, was worked on. |
| **Effort alignment** | How closely the hours matched the weights you both agreed. |
| **Timesheet compliance** | Whether they filled the timesheet at all. |

### Three things the screen will do that surprise people

**"Ambiguous".** An item whose words matched **more than one** KRA is given to
**neither**, and both candidates are named. The system will not guess between
two of your KRAs — that is your call. These are the items to clear first,
because they hold hours hostage.

**"Not measured from timesheets".** A KRA can be declared unmeasurable from
logged hours — a customer-satisfaction score, say — with the reason shown
beside it. Such a KRA is **not offered in the dropdown**, so you cannot park
hours against it by accident. *Today that declaration is set in the data by
HR, not from this screen.*

**"This person has no KRAs for this cycle".** No amount of mapping will help.
Their objectives have to exist before their hours can be placed against them
— go back to **Team KRA Sheets**.

### Closed months, and the override

Once HR closes a period it appears under **Closed months**. A settled month
**keeps the numbers it was closed with even if a KRA is reweighted or an item
is remapped afterwards** — that is what makes it safe to carry into
calibration.

If a settled month is wrong, you can **Override** it (the button reads
**Change** on a month already overridden):

1. Enter the score, or the grade, you believe is right.
2. **Give the reason.** The box is on the screen before you save and the save will not go through without it.
3. Save.

The reason then shows against that month, with your name on it, to you and to
the employee. **Remove the override** puts the computed figure back.

Use this for what the timesheet could not have known — work done in a client's
own tracker, a secondment, a month of firefighting nobody logged. Do not use
it to smooth a number you simply dislike: the override is visible, attributed
and permanent in the record.

## 8. What is not yours

Worth knowing so you do not go looking:

- **Salary and increments.** Increment Simulation sits behind its own permission, held by HR. You will not see it, and that is deliberate.
- **Publishing ratings.** HR publishes; until then your report's *My Rating* stays greyed out.
- **The 9-Box grid** is visible to HR and HOD only. Your *Potential* rating feeds it; you do not see the grid.
- **Closing a timesheet period.** HR does that. You override afterwards if you must.
- **The KRA Library.** HR publishes the shelves; your reports pick from them.

## 9. Common questions

**A report's reporting line changed to me mid-cycle.** Their open records moved
with them. You do not need to recreate anything.

**"Access denied" on a page.** The message names the permission that was
missing — send that wording to HR rather than describing the screen.

**My report says their coverage is wrong.** Look at the mapping first: an
unplaced or ambiguous item is the usual cause, and it is yours to fix. If
hours are genuinely missing, that is a timesheet-export problem and they fix
it by re-uploading — re-uploading the same export changes nothing, so it is
always safe to try.

**A month is settled and the number is wrong.** Override it with a reason.
Do not ask HR to re-close the period; a settled month is meant to hold still.

**The evaluation screen shows no cycle.** No cycle is open. HR opens them.

**Nothing appears under my team.** Your reports are read from the employee
master. If somebody is missing there, they are missing here — HR owns that
import.
