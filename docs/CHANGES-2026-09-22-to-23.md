## 2. The interface

This is the change you see before any other, and the one this document
under-reported in its first version: four one-line rows in the table above
with no section and no screenshots. It is written out properly here.

### The shell: a left sidebar became role tabs

The 29-item left sidebar was replaced by four **role tabs** across the top,
each opening its own row of pages. Every tab carries its group's colour —
tinted when closed, fully saturated when open — and its own icon, so the row
reads as four places rather than one live tab and three dead ones.

Which tabs you get is decided by `core.page_permission`, not a hardcoded role
list, and an empty group disappears rather than opening onto nothing.

**Employee — 2 tabs: My Performance, Engagement & People**

![Employee — 2 tabs: My Performance, Engagement & People](screens/ui-shell-employee.png)

**Manager — 3 tabs, + Team**

![Manager — 3 tabs, + Team](screens/ui-shell-manager.png)

**HR / Admin — 4 tabs, + HR Admin. Fifteen pages, wrapped onto two rows**

![HR / Admin — 4 tabs, + HR Admin. Fifteen pages, wrapped onto two rows](screens/ui-shell-admin.png)


HR Admin's fifteen pages need 2,286px at a 1,440px window. The sub-nav
therefore **wraps** rather than scrolling sideways — measured, not guessed. A
scrolling row hid eight of HR's pages off the right edge with no cue they
existed, which would have been worse than the sidebar it replaced on exactly
the axis the sidebar was good at.

The layout was built **both ways first** — a left-sidebar variant and a
role-tab variant, from one generator — so the choice was made by looking at
them rather than by argument, and the round trip back to a sidebar was proven
to work before committing to tabs.

### One hero band instead of 29 header rows

Every page had hand-rolled its own title row. There is now one `PageHead`
component: a coloured band carrying the title, a subtitle and whatever chips
or buttons the page needs. It takes a hue, so a page announces which group it
belongs to before you have read the title — navy for My Performance, teal for
Team, violet for HR Admin, green for Engagement & People.

### One search control, on eleven lists

Lists that ran to hundreds of rows had no way to find anything. `SearchBox`
is one control with one behaviour, added to eleven of them — and it hides
itself when the list is empty, rather than offering to search nothing.

### Status tabs

`StatusTabs` replaces "a count in the header and one long list". A manager's
Team KRA Sheets page now opens on **Pending your review**, with Approved,
Returned and Not submitted beside it. Tabs with a count of zero are hidden.
Before this, an approved sheet looked identical to one nobody had touched
until you opened it.

**The role tabs, the hero band, the status tabs and the search control, together on one page**

![The role tabs, the hero band, the status tabs and the search control, together on one page](screens/ui-page-manager.png)


### All Approvals

A single queue of every pending decision in the company, for HR. Two kinds of
row, and the page is honest about the difference: a KRA sheet or a growth plan
has been *submitted* and gets an Approve button; a mid-year sign-off or an
evaluation is waiting on somebody to *write* something, so those rows show who
they are waiting on and nothing else. Offering an Approve button there would
mean writing an empty evaluation in someone else's name.

**Calibration — a hero in a different hue, on a dense table page**

### The palette

Sampled from the client's own reference screenshots rather than invented:
navy `#1b3b6f`, pink `#ec407a`, teal `#17a2b8`, amber `#f5821f`, green
`#43a047`, plus violet for HR and AI surfaces. Pink is **no longer** the
active-nav colour — the active item is coloured by its group instead, which
is what tells you where you are in a 29-item menu; one accent colour could
not carry that.

## 3. The dashboard

Signing in used to drop you onto My KRAs whatever the cycle was doing and
whatever you owed. It now lands on a dashboard built to the Humanware
reference you sent: a strip of stat cards, a single action banner, solid
colour "desk" tiles for what is outstanding, then quick links.

**Every number is a real count.** There is no filler tile. New queries back
the figures that did not exist: growth goals, connects logged, open connect
action items, reports with no 1-on-1, and KRA sheets submitted but undecided.

**A tile only exists when its count does.** Your reference shows six tiles
even when four read `00`; this does not, because a wall of zeroes trains
people to stop reading the row.

### Two bugs this turned up

1. **A brand-new employee was told "Nothing is waiting on you."** The
   guard required a `pms.kra_sheets` row to exist, so someone who had never
   opened the product fell through every branch to the all-clear. The one
   person who most needs telling was the one not told.
2. **In manager evaluation the KRA queue is empty** (sheets are approved by
   then), so a manager owing an assessment for every report got the same
   all-clear. It now says "3 evaluations to write".

Both have regression tests, and each bug was put back to confirm the test
fails against it before the test was trusted.

## 4. Access: an employee could see the HR Admin tab

**This was a live fault, not a preference.** The sign-in path stored the user
object from the *login* response, which carries no `pages` field, and the nav
reads a missing `pages` as "this tenant has not configured page permissions —
show everything". That is the deliberate fallback for an unconfigured tenant,
and it fired for everyone. For the whole of a first session **every role saw
every tab**. It corrected itself on the next refresh, which is why it went
unnoticed: reloading is the first thing anybody does.

The API always enforced permissions, so no data was exposed — but the menu
was wrong, and a menu that offers HR Admin to an employee is not acceptable.

**The data was never wrong.** Checked on the live tenant before touching any
code: an employee resolves to 12 public pages, none of them HR Admin. Only
the client was wrong. `/me` is now the single way a user object is built, on
a cold load and after sign-in alike, and nothing renders until it answers.

Verified by signing in through the real form as all four roles and comparing
the tabs before and after a refresh:

| Role | Tabs |
|---|---|
| Employee | My Performance · Engagement & People |
| Manager | My Performance · Team · Engagement & People |
| Delivery Head | My Performance · Team · HR Admin (1) · Engagement & People |
| Admin | My Performance · Team · HR Admin (15) · Engagement & People |

**Improvement Plans moved** from Team to My Performance. It was the only item
left in Team for an employee, because it is deliberately public — row-scoped
in the handler, so you see your own plan. Gating it would have taken an
employee's own improvement plan away from them to tidy a tab.

### Requests on both dashboards

A submission is one row that is a **sent request** to the employee and a
**pending request** to their manager. Both ends read the same rows with the
same status tests — the only way the two can agree.

Requested format, adopted from your own sheet:

> single table instead of separate with the same parameter displayed separate.

The parameter is a **merged cell spanning its KRAs**, so it is written once
however many sit under it.

![My KRAs — editable (draft or returned)](screens/kra-table-mykras-edit.png)

**My KRAs — locked (submitted or approved)**

![My KRAs — locked (submitted or approved)](screens/kra-table-mykras-readonly.png)

**A manager reviewing the same sheet**

![A manager reviewing the same sheet](screens/kra-table-manager-review.png)

**Phone, 390px — the merged column folds instead of scrolling sideways**

![Phone, 390px — the merged column folds instead of scrolling sideways](screens/kra-table-narrow.png)


**Four screens, not the two asked for.** The manager's review shows the same
KRAs; leaving it as cards would have broken the format the moment a manager
opened it.

**The editable sheet needed a new control.** With the parameter merged,
changing a KRA's parameter physically moves the row — so the per-row select
became **"Move to…"**. It never displays the current parameter, because the
merged cell already does and repeating it on every row is the exact
duplication this change removes. The current parameter is also left out of
its own options.

**A merged column cannot survive a phone.** Below 720px the table becomes
blocks, which makes `rowSpan` inert, so the parameter is a full-width band
ahead of its KRAs. Measured at 390px: the KRA field is 328px wide and the
page does not scroll sideways. The desktop widths leaked through on the first
attempt and squeezed that field to about eight characters.

**"Measures" is now "KPIs"** wherever a user reads it, matching the column
name the upload template has always used. Labels only, no data change.

## 6. Auto-mail on submission

`notify()` takes `{ email: true }`, and the submission and decision events
set it: KRA sheet, growth plan, self-appraisal, mid-year sign-off, and the
approve/return coming back the other way.

**Opt-in, not automatic for every notification.** The in-app bell carries
plenty nobody needs an email about; mailing all of it would train people to
filter the address, and then the submissions would be missed too.

An SMTP provider is wired (nodemailer), reading host/port/user/password from
the tenant's settings first and the environment second — **so it goes live
the moment those exist, with no redeploy.**

> **Currently in simulated mode.** Nothing leaves the machine. Every attempt
> is written to `core.notif_log` with its outcome. Send SMTP details and it
> switches on.

A mail failure never fails the submission: the work is already saved and the
in-app notification already written.

## 7. Other fixes worth naming

**A KRA shelf published under a department was invisible.** Reported as
"after changing department and designation, new KRAs were not fetched".
Reproduced, and it was not caching — the server re-reads designation on every
request. Three queries look for a shelf: the department asked for, the
department the employee is in (only when department matching is on), then the
company-wide shelf, which *requires* the department column to be blank. A
library uploaded with departments filled in, on a tenant at the default
scope, had no query that could reach it. The picker then said *"No KRA
library has been published for Cloud Engineer"* while two rows for Cloud
Engineer sat in the table — a false empty state, which is worse than a
missing one: it tells HR their upload did not land.

**Department on the Career Pathing Matrix**, carried end to end rather than
added to the spreadsheet alone. Blank means every department; a named one
applies only there and beats a company-wide rung for the same move.

**Manager potential rating.** Potential was recorded only at calibration,
which is months after the manager who knows the person best has formed a
view. It is now captured with the manager's evaluation as well, and shown on
the calibration screen as the starting point for that conversation — not as
the answer. Calibration still sets the 9-box cell; the two are kept apart so
"why did this change" stays answerable.

**A null employee in the approvals queue.** Two queries read `employee_id`
from the LEFT-JOINed side, so rows arrived with no employee and duplicate
React keys. The filter then showed the wrong rows.
